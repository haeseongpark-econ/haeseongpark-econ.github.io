Job Market Paper

Agglomeration Beyond Productivity: Economic Organization and Resilience to Natural Disasters

This paper examines how differences in economic structure shape the short-run economic impacts of natural disasters on cities. Using wind strength to proxy disaster intensity and electricity consumption to measure economic activity, I show that natural disasters reduce aggregate economic activity at the metropolitan statistical area level.

I find substantial heterogeneity across labor market concentration, transportation network centralization, and local trade linkages. Industry concentration amplifies disaster losses, whereas centralized road networks and greater internal goods sourcing improve resilience.
